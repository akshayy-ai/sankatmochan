## Incident 1: Cyclone Fani — Odisha (May 2019)

**What happened:** Cyclone Fani hit Puri district. Villagers in Kuapada received *zero warning* — no mobile alerts, no sirens. Power was cut 2 days before. Government kept changing predicted landfall time. **Seshadeba Nayak lost his 9-year-old daughter Payal** — speech and hearing impaired — when a brick wall collapsed. Of 39 deaths in Puri, most were attributed to communication failures. The nearest cyclone shelter was 3 km away and could hold only 500 for a population of 4,000+.

**How Sankatmochan helps:** Voice-based warnings in **Odia and tribal dialects** via automated calls — not text-based alerts that require literacy and connectivity. The AI processes SOS calls in any language and routes to NDRF with location.

*Sources: Hindustan Times, Down To Earth*

## Incident 2: Kerala Floods — Fishermen as Last Resort (August 2018)

**What happened:** Worst flooding in a century. **500+ dead, 1.8 million displaced.** Official NDRF teams — composed of Hindi-speaking personnel from multiple states — couldn't communicate with **Malayalam-speaking victims**. Local fishermen who spoke the language ended up rescuing **65,000 people** because they could actually talk to the stranded families. Without these fishermen, the death toll would have been far higher.

**How Sankatmochan helps:** Real-time **Hindi ↔ Malayalam translation** on the 112 line. NDRF teams could communicate with victims as effectively as local fishermen. Distress calls in Malayalam get processed with GPS coordinates and routed to the nearest responder — no bilingual volunteer needed.

*Sources: Onmanorama, UCL Research*

## Incident 3: Wayanad Landslides — Tribal Communities Ignored (July 2024)

**What happened:** Landslides in Mundakkai and Chooralmala killed **420 people**, injured 397, left 118 missing — Kerala's deadliest disaster. The victims included **Paniya tribal communities** and tea plantation workers with limited literacy. A landslide warning was issued **16 hours before** the event — but it never reached these communities. The warning system failed at the "last mile" for the most vulnerable people. Forest officials later rescued 6 tribals including 4 children from the forests.

**How Sankatmochan helps:** Voice warnings in **Paniya and tribal languages** — not text-based alerts in Malayalam or English that these communities can't read. Automated calls explaining danger and evacuation routes in their mother tongue.

*Sources: Wikipedia, India TV News, SphereIndia Assessment*

## Incident 4: Chennai Floods — Migrant Workers Stranded (Dec 2015)

**What happened:** 1.8 million affected, **470+ dead.** Migrant workers from Bihar, Odisha, and Bengal were stranded at Chennai Central Railway Station — sleeping on floors, surviving on biscuits. **900 Bihari workers** couldn't communicate with Tamil-speaking rescue authorities. They received only one-word instructions in broken Hindi: "sit, stand, line up." Workers refused food and water, wanting only to leave. NGO volunteers had to be brought in specifically to bridge the language gap.

**How Sankatmochan helps:** Real-time **Hindi-Tamil-Bengali-Odia translation** on the 112 helpline. Migrant workers describe their situation in Bhojpuri or Bengali — the AI translates and routes to Tamil Nadu emergency services with location. Evacuation instructions broadcast in **all languages simultaneously**.

*Sources: The News Minute, Middle East Institute*

## Incident 5: Chamoli Glacier Burst — Multi-State Workers Trapped (Feb 2021)

**What happened:** Glacier burst killed or left missing **204 people**. Only 77 were from Uttarakhand — the rest were migrant construction workers from Bihar, Punjab, Haryana, Kashmir, and UP. **35 workers were trapped in a tunnel** at the Tapovan-Vishnugad hydroelectric project. They spoke Bhojpuri, Punjabi, Kashmiri, and various Hindi dialects. The multi-state, multi-language workforce made rescue coordination extremely difficult.

**How Sankatmochan helps:** Trapped workers send **voice SOS in Bhojpuri, Punjabi, or Kashmiri** — instantly translated for NDRF/ITBP rescue coordinators. Families across multiple states call in their own languages to report missing workers. The AI creates a unified case regardless of language.

*Sources: PreventionWeb, CNN*

## Incident 6: Northeast Migrants Attacked in Bangalore (2012-2014)

**What happened:** In Bangalore, men attacked a **Manipuri student with rocks** for not speaking Kannada, yelling "this is India, not China." Part of a broader pattern — **30,000+ Northeast Indians fled Bangalore** in August 2012. Victims speaking Manipuri, Mizo, or Naga languages **couldn't communicate with Kannada-speaking police** to report attacks or seek help.

**How Sankatmochan helps:** The AI processes distress calls in **Manipuri, Mizo, or any Northeast language**, translates to the local dispatch language, and routes emergency response. Victims describe their situation in their own language — no barrier preventing them from seeking help.

*Sources: Hindustan Times*

## Incident 7: Manipur Ethnic Violence — Communication Breakdown (2023-2026)

**What happened:** Ethnic violence between Meitei and Kuki-Zo communities: **258 dead, 60,000 displaced, 4,786 houses burnt.** Meitei speak Meiteilon; Kuki-Zo speak Thadou, Hmar, Vaiphei, and other Kuki languages. During active violence, the 112 system's Hindi/English default created barriers for distressed callers. Internet shutdowns made it worse.

**How Sankatmochan helps:** Processes emergency calls in **Meiteilon, Thadou, Hmar, and Vaiphei**. During internet shutdowns, the **voice-based AI on the 112 line uses cellular voice** (not data) — it remains functional when all other communication channels are blocked.

*Sources: Wikipedia, Human Rights Watch*

## Incident 8: Odisha Tribal Communities — Systemic Exclusion from Warnings

**What happened:** Despite Odisha reducing cyclone deaths from **10,000+ (1999)** to double digits in later cyclones, a persistent "last-mile" gap remains. Tribal communities speaking **Sora, Ho, Bonda, and Juang** — none served by the 112 helpline — are systematically excluded from early warning systems. India has sent **134 billion SMS alerts** in 19+ languages during disasters, but tribal and minority languages remain excluded.

**How Sankatmochan helps:** Processes and delivers warnings in **Sora, Ho, Bonda, Juang** via voice calls. Instead of text alerts in Odia or English that these communities can't read, the system delivers **spoken alerts in their mother tongue** with clear evacuation instructions.

*Sources: OSDMA, India Water Portal, PIB*
